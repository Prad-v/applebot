Applebot Features User guide Changelog

User guide

Applebot is a chat assistant that runs entirely on your iPhone. It works in airplane mode and never sends your messages or documents anywhere. The only exception is a connector you choose to switch on (see below), which talks directly from your iPhone to its own service.

Requirements

  • iOS 26 or later
  • An iPhone with Apple Intelligence: iPhone 15 Pro, 15 Pro Max, or any iPhone 16 or newer
  • Apple Intelligence turned on in Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri. The first time, iOS downloads the model; Applebot shows a banner until it is ready.

Chatting

  1. Open the Chats tab and tap the compose button (or Start chatting). Each new chat is a brand new conversation with no memory of other chats.
  2. Type a message and tap the arrow. The answer streams in as it is generated. Tap the stop button to interrupt.
  3. Long-press an answer to copy or share it.
  4. Swipe a chat right to pin it, or left to delete it. Use the search field to find chats by title or content.

Start a new conversation

Tap the compose button in the chat's top bar to start a fresh conversation at any time. Your current chat is saved in the list, and the new one starts empty: no history, no summary, and document search reset to all documents. The button is greyed out while the current chat is still empty, and a chat that never receives a message is not saved.

Long conversations are handled automatically: older messages are condensed into a short summary so the model can keep going within its small context window.

Conversation style and emotional chat

In Settings → Conversation style, choose how Applebot talks:

  • Neutral (default): concise and factual.
  • Warm companion: kind and attentive. It listens first and acknowledges how you feel.
  • Supportive coach: encouraging and practical, with one small next step.

In the warm and coach styles, Applebot reads the mood of each message on your iPhone and adapts. If you are clearly upset it skips the document search so it can simply talk with you. Empty chats ask "How are you feeling today?".

Applebot is not a therapist. If you are in crisis, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line.

Connectors: Gmail and Calendar

Connectors let Applebot look things up in your accounts when you ask, and propose actions that you approve first. They are off until you connect them.

  1. Open Settings → Connected services → Connectors.
  2. Tap Connect Calendar or Connect Gmail and follow the prompts. Keep Use in chats on, or switch it off to pause a connector without signing out.
  3. Ask in a chat. For example:
    You askWhat happens
    "What's on my calendar tomorrow?"Your calendar is read and summarised
    "Any emails from Alice this week?"Gmail is searched and the results are summarised
    "Reply to Alice's lunch email saying yes"Applebot writes a reply and shows you the draft to approve
    "Add lunch with Sam tomorrow at 12:30"An approval sheet shows the event; it is added only if you approve

Approvals

Reading happens straight away. Anything that changes something, such as saving a Gmail draft or adding a calendar event, shows a sheet with the exact content (recipient, subject and text, or the event's title, time and place). It only happens if you tap the green button. Don't allow, dismissing the sheet, or leaving the chat all mean no.

Applebot never sends email. It can only save a draft in Gmail for you to review and send yourself.

Connectors and documents

Connectors work in chats that are not searching your documents. If you have documents in your library, a notice in the chat offers Use Gmail and Calendar instead.

Setting up Gmail

Calendar needs no setup. Gmail needs a one-time Google registration: create an OAuth client of type iOS with bundle ID dev.kekaflow.applebot in Google Cloud Console, enable the Gmail API, add yourself as a test user, then build with GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID=<your id> make project. Full steps, limits and the security model are in docs/connectors.md. Google test-mode sign-ins last 7 days, after which you connect again.

Chat with your documents

Add documents

  1. Open the Documents tab and tap +.
  2. Choose Import files (PDF, text, Markdown, RTF, CSV, JSON, YAML, or images such as photos of paper documents) or Paste text.
  3. Applebot reads the text, splits it into passages, and indexes them on the device. Scanned PDFs and images use on-device text recognition (OCR).

Re-importing the same content is detected and skipped. Originals are not copied into the app; only extracted text and search vectors are stored.

Attach documents to a chat

Tap the + button next to the message box:

OptionWhat it does
Files…Pick PDFs, text, Markdown, and other supported files
Photos…Pick photos or screenshots of documents; text is read with on-device OCR
Paste text…Add a note by pasting text
From my library…Choose documents you already imported

Imported documents are added to your library and attached to this chat. They appear as chips above the message box, and this chat then searches only the attached documents. Tap a chip to change the selection, or tap its x to remove it. Removing every attached document goes back to searching all documents. Other chats are not affected.

Ask questions

  • With documents in your library, new chats use them automatically. The chat's toolbar menu has Use my documents to turn this on or off.
  • Choose Search all documents or pick specific documents to restrict the search.
  • Answers cite passages like [1]. Tap a source chip, or Why this answer?, to see the search query and every passage given to the model.
  • If the documents do not contain the answer, Applebot says so instead of guessing. A small on-device model can still make mistakes, so check important facts against the source.

Specific questions work best ("What is the late fee on invoices?"). For broad requests, choose one document in the search scope and ask for a summary.

Privacy and security

  • Fully on-device. No servers, accounts, or analytics. With connectors off there are no network requests at all. A connector you switch on talks directly from your iPhone to its own service (for example Google) and nowhere else.
  • Connector safety. Sign-in uses Google's secure sheet (Applebot never sees your password) and the token is kept in the iOS Keychain. Email text is treated as untrusted, and nothing is changed without your approval.
  • Encrypted at rest. Chats and the document library live in a file-protected folder.
  • App lock. In Settings → Privacy, turn on Require Face ID / passcode to lock the app whenever it goes to the background.
  • Delete everything. Settings → Delete all chats and documents erases all data Applebot stores and signs out of connected accounts.

Limits to be aware of

  • The on-device model is much smaller than cloud chatbots. It is best at summarizing, rewriting, extracting facts from your documents, brainstorming, and simple Q&A. It is weak at math, code, and recent world knowledge.
  • Its context window is about 4,000 tokens, shared by the conversation, retrieved passages, and the answer.
  • The model may decline some requests because of Apple's safety guardrails. Applebot then asks you to put it another way.
